The phrase "we have checked everything obvious" is doing a lot of work here, and it's where I want to apply pressure. A gradual decline with normal-looking error distribution and no correlating change is the signature of a problem you can't see because you're aggregating it away. Let me reason from root cause.

## Reframe the symptom

"More of everything failing" with a stable error distribution is itself a clue. A single broken thing produces a *spike in one error type*. A flat 1.4-point erosion across all error types over three weeks means the population you're measuring is *shifting*, not that any one code path broke. The failure isn't in your system — it's in the mix of traffic flowing through it. **Stop looking at the system; start looking at the inputs.**

## The decomposition: a rate is a ratio, and ratios move for two reasons

Success rate = successes / attempts. It can fall because the numerator degrades (something got worse) OR because the denominator's composition changed (the same code now sees harder traffic). You've exhaustively checked the first. The evidence — gradual, uncorrelated, evenly distributed — points hard at the second. **Validate that assumption before building on it.** Don't debug the payment path further until you've ruled out a population shift.

## What to actually do — disaggregate before you debug

The single biggest mistake here is continuing to investigate the aggregate. A blended 97.8% is a weighted average hiding a mix. Re-slice the *same* data you already have along every dimension before touching code:

- **Cohort the decline by time.** Is every segment down 1.4 points, or is one segment cratering while the rest hold flat? Almost always it's the latter. Find the segment.
- **Slice by:** card BIN / issuing bank, card network, country/region, currency, payment method, customer type (new vs returning), card-on-file vs fresh entry, app version / SDK version, retry vs first-attempt, and amount band.
- **Plot each slice's volume alongside its success rate.** You're hunting for one of two shapes:
  1. A segment whose *success rate* is dropping (a specific issuer tightening rules, a specific BIN range, a partner who silently changed behavior even if their status page is green).
  2. A segment whose *volume* is growing while its success rate is merely *normal-but-low* — a marketing campaign, new geography, or new cohort (e.g. more new users, more of a high-decline card type) mechanically dragging the blended average down with zero change to any code path.

Shape 2 is the one teams miss for weeks, because nothing is "broken." Three weeks of gradual decline matches the ramp of a campaign, an A/B rollout, an SEO/affiliate channel, or a pricing change pulling in a different customer mix.

## Highest-probability root causes, ranked by fit to your evidence

1. **Traffic-mix shift (denominator).** A growing low-quality cohort — new market, new acquisition channel, more first-time/no-CVV/prepaid cards, more retries. Best fit for "gradual + no deploy + normal error distribution."
2. **Silent issuer/network tightening.** Banks adjust fraud models continuously; no status page reports it. Shows up as rising soft-declines (insufficient funds, do-not-honor, fraud suspicion) concentrated in specific BINs. Check the *issuer decline reason codes*, not your own error buckets.
3. **A slow-moving internal drift that doesn't look like a deploy:** an expiring cert/token rolling out gradually, a cache or feature flag at increasing rollout %, a data field that's been degrading (e.g. a 3DS or device-fingerprint signal quietly going stale), or a dependency you ramped via config not deploy. Config and flag changes won't show in your deploy correlation — check those changelogs separately.
4. **Your own funnel upstream.** More attempts reaching the payment step that were never going to succeed (validation loosened, a "retry payment" button added, an abandoned-cart re-prompt). Same code, worse inputs.

## Concrete next steps

1. Pull issuer/gateway **decline reason codes** for the failing transactions and trend each code over the three weeks. One or two codes will be carrying the whole 1.4 points. That single chart usually ends the investigation.
2. Build the **per-segment success-rate-and-volume** view above. Find the cohort that diverges from the baseline date.
3. Once you have the diverging segment, ask "what started ramping three weeks ago that feeds this segment" — campaign, channel, geo, flag, A/B test, partner config. That's your `why`.

## Two challenges to your framing

- **"No deploys correlate" is a weaker signal than it feels.** Most gradual payment regressions come from config, feature flags, gradual rollouts, partner-side changes, or traffic shifts — none of which appear in deploy history. The absence of a deploy correlation is exactly what you'd expect for the most likely causes. Don't let it steer you away.
- **Decide whether 1.4 points is even worth a deep hunt.** If the diverging segment turns out to be a new, structurally-lower-converting market you intentionally opened, the "regression" is just an accounting artifact of a mix change — and the right move is to *segment your SLO by cohort* so a healthy growth decision stops looking like an outage. Verify the cause before you decide it's a problem to fix.

The one-liner: **disaggregate the rate before debugging the system — a flat, gradual, uncorrelated decline is a population shift until proven otherwise, and decline reason codes plus per-segment trends will tell you which population in about an hour.**
