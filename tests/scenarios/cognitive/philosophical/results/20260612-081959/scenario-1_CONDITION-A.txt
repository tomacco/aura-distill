The honest answer first: **with the inputs as you've stated them, this is a coin flip — and that's the most important finding, not a problem to solve.**

You told me both options work, budget and timeline are identical, and the team is split 50-50. If all of that is true, you've already done the hard part: you've eliminated the bad option (there isn't one). What's left is not an engineering decision, it's a coordination decision. So let me apply first principles to break it open, because I don't think the inputs are actually as symmetric as they look.

**1. Trace each choice to failure modes, not features.** Don't compare "audit trail" vs "simpler." Compare what breaks and how badly. Event sourcing + CQRS has more moving parts: eventual consistency means you will ship a class of bugs that simply cannot exist in Option B — read-after-write anomalies, projection lag, replay/version-skew when event schemas evolve. CRUD + CDC has fewer failure modes and the ones it has are well-trodden. *The principle "choose the option with fewer failure modes" points at B,* unless you have a concrete requirement that demands A.

**2. So validate the assumption "both options work."** That premise is doing all the load-bearing work, and I'd challenge it. They don't work *equally* — they work for *different requirements*. The real question you haven't stated: **do you have a hard requirement for a full audit trail or temporal replay?** Regulatory audit, "show me the exact state of the system at timestamp T," debugging by replaying production event streams — if any of those is a real, named requirement, then B doesn't actually work and this stops being a tie. If none of them is real, you're buying event-sourcing's complexity on spec.

**3. Reversibility is the tiebreaker the team is missing.** This is the decisive asymmetry. You can migrate **CRUD → event sourcing** later (add an event log, build projections, backfill). Migrating **event sourcing → CRUD** is brutal — you've spread eventual-consistency assumptions through every consumer and you can't un-ring that bell cheaply. The options are *not* equally reversible. Prefer the decision you can undo. **That points at B**, and lets you adopt A later for the specific bounded context that genuinely needs it, with evidence in hand instead of a 50-50 guess.

**4. Second-order effects, 6 months out.** Event sourcing changes how every future engineer reasons about *every* read and write — it's a system-wide cognitive tax, not a localized one. Ask: does the whole system need that, or do one or two contexts? If it's one or two, system-wide event sourcing fails the isolation principle — you'd be coupling the whole platform to a constraint a fraction of it needs.

**5. On the 50-50 split:** don't break it by vote, seniority, or who argues hardest — those are the worst possible tiebreakers and they leave half the team unconvinced. Break it by surfacing the missing premise (the audit/replay requirement in #2). Once that's named, the split usually collapses on its own, because the two camps are almost always optimizing for different unstated requirements.

**My recommendation:** Default to **Option B (CRUD + CDC)** unless you can name a concrete, current requirement for audit trail or temporal replay. Fewer failure modes, strong consistency, and — most importantly — it's the reversible choice that keeps the door to event sourcing open for the specific context that earns it later. Adopt the simpler thing now; let evidence, not a tie-breaking vote, pull you toward complexity.

One caveat worth stating plainly: if that audit/replay requirement *does* exist and is real, ignore most of the above — A wins, and the "simplicity" argument for B becomes a false premise. The whole decision hinges on that one question, which is the question your 50-50 split is actually avoiding. Go answer it first.
