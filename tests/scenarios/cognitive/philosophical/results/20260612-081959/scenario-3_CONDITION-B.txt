You've checked everything obvious, which means the answer is almost certainly in something you've been *treating as constant* and therefore not measuring. Here's how I'd attack it.

## First, reframe what the data is telling you

A gradual decline with normal error distribution and "more of everything failing" is the signature of a **population shift, not a system fault**. Nothing broke. Instead, the *mix* of traffic flowing through your unchanged system is slowly changing, and the new mix has a structurally lower success rate. Your system is a constant; the input distribution is the variable.

This is why no deploy correlates — there's nothing to correlate against. You're looking for a step function and the cause is a ramp.

## Bracket your assumptions (the thing you must do first)

Suspend the belief that "everything is normal except success rate." That belief is what's hiding the cause. The error distribution looks normal *in aggregate* — that's exactly what averaging away a shift looks like. **Stop looking at the aggregate. Segment relentlessly.**

Cut the 1.4-point drop across every dimension you can, comparing 3 weeks ago vs. now:

- **BIN / card brand / issuing bank / country** — a single large issuer tightening its fraud rules, or growth in a geography with worse auth rates, produces exactly this curve.
- **Card type** — debit vs. credit, prepaid share rising.
- **Payment method mix** — a lower-converting method (a new wallet, a region's local method) growing as a share of total.
- **New vs. returning customers** — acquisition campaign or SEO/marketing shift pulling in a worse-converting cohort.
- **Amount buckets** — higher-value txns fail more (fraud thresholds).
- **Retry traffic** — a client/app change that increased automatic retries inflates the *denominator* with attempts that were always doomed.
- **Specific decline codes** — "normal distribution" almost never survives contact with raw decline reason codes segmented by issuer. Pull the actual code mix (insufficient funds vs. do-not-honor vs. soft fraud declines) and trend each one. One of them is rising.

**The key question:** is *each segment's* success rate stable while the *mix* shifted toward bad segments (population shift)? Or did *one specific segment's* rate degrade (a real, localized fault)? Those have completely different fixes, and the aggregate can't distinguish them. This is the whole ballgame.

## Falsify, don't confirm

Form one hypothesis from the segmentation and try to *kill* it, not confirm it. If you think "new-customer growth is the cause," the falsification test is: hold the cohort mix constant at 3-weeks-ago proportions and recompute the blended rate. If the drop disappears, the cause is mix. If 1.4 points of decline survives that reweighting, mix is *not* your answer and you move on. A hypothesis you can't disprove here is just a story.

## A few non-obvious culprits worth pre-morteming

- **Silent partner drift.** "No partner status issues" means no *outages*. It does not mean a processor or issuer didn't quietly tune fraud/risk models — these roll out gradually and never show on a status page. Ask your processor directly for *their* view of your auth-rate trend by issuer.
- **Expiring artifacts.** Network tokens, stored-credential expirations, or a cohort of cards aging past expiry — anything with a slow rolling clock produces a ramp, not a spike.
- **Certificate / config slow-rot** that degrades rather than fails (e.g., a fallback path silently engaging more often).
- **Retry/idempotency change** inflating doomed attempts in the denominator — success *rate* drops while successful *count* is unchanged.

## What "in your control" means here

You can't control issuer fraud policy or the traffic mix the market sends you. You *can* control retry logic, routing, 3DS/SCA invocation, network-token coverage, and which processor handles which BINs. Once segmentation tells you *where* the drop lives, the fix is almost always a routing or retry adjustment for that segment — not a hunt for a bug that doesn't exist.

## Concrete next step

Build one table: success rate by **(issuer × payment method × new/returning)**, this week vs. 3 weeks ago, with volume share for each cell. The 1.4 points is concentrated somewhere — either in cells whose *share grew* (mix problem → fix routing/retries for that segment) or cells whose *rate fell* (localized fault → that's your bug). You almost certainly already have this data; you've just been viewing it aggregated. De-aggregate and the cause will surface.

If after full segmentation the decline is genuinely uniform across every cell — same drop everywhere, no mix shift — then say so explicitly, because that's a different and rarer animal (a global factor like a shared fraud-score vendor or a network-level rule change), and it's worth stating honestly rather than forcing a narrative onto it.
