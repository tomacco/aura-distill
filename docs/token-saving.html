Token Saving

Feature hub · updated July 2026 · every claim links to published research

Distill's job is to make your Claude remember. This page tracks a second job it's taking on: making your Claude cheaper to run — without ever trading away quality behind your back. What ships here is measured first, published with raw data, and always yours to switch off.

The news, first

Distill now installs two lightweight subagent presets — scribe and scout. They make the helper agents Claude Code spawns for you dramatically cheaper to start: a text-only job (judging, summarizing, classifying) boots at ~2,000 tokens instead of ~19,000–27,000. That's it. No behavior change, no data collected, two small text files on your disk, one command to remove them.

What changes for you

Nothing you have to learn. When Claude Code delegates work to a helper (it does this more than most people realize — search sweeps, reviews, summaries), it can now pick a preset that matches the job's shape instead of always spawning a fully-equipped generalist:

  • scribe — for jobs that only read the prompt and return text. Carries no tools at all.
  • scout — for find-and-report jobs. Carries read-only tools (search and read; it cannot modify anything — a safety property, not just a cost one).
  • generalist — the built-in default, unchanged, still there for open-ended work.

You'll simply see "scribe" or "scout" in the spawn line where you used to see a generic agent — and a smaller number in your usage.

Under the hood: what a helper actually costs

Most people use Claude Code + distill without seeing what a "simple" subagent carries on its back. Every helper is a fresh Claude instance, and before it reads the first word of your task, its context is already loaded with scaffolding — the biggest slab of which is tool schemas: the formal descriptions of every tool it could theoretically use (shell, file editing, browser…), shipped in full even if the job never touches one.

anatomy of a helper's starting context — measured on real spawns, July 2026

The schemas aren't waste when the job needs tools. They're pure tax when it doesn't — and judging, summarizing, extracting and drafting never do. The presets simply stop paying for capabilities the job can't use. Your main session is untouched; this is only about the helpers.

The evidence

Measured on real spawns with an identical minimal task, July 2026 (raw JSON in the research repo):

14x lighter start for text-only jobs; ~3.5x for read-only exploration

Context for the numbers: mining 40 days of the maintainer's own usage (33 sessions, 3,360 requests) showed that ~85% of token cost is moving context around, not generating answers. Helper spawns pay that tax at birth — which is why trimming their starting context is the rare lever that costs nothing in quality. Full analysis: The Token Economics of Remembering.

The research line: shipped, in research, and honestly disproven

Token saving is a research program, not a settings page. The release rule, set by the maintainer: an optimization ships silently only if it provably changes nothing about distill's output. Anything that trades tokens against something else becomes an explicit, documented, opt-in knob — or doesn't ship. Here's the current state of every line:

Shipped Agent presets (scribe/scout). Free win: same outputs, 14x/3.5x lighter starts, off switch included. You're reading its announcement.
In research Learned spawn profiles. Distill retrospectives could learn, from your own sessions, which job shapes route to which preset and which model tier — cheap models for mechanical work, frontier for judgment. Early trials: mechanical transforms and extraction survived the cheapest tier untouched (the only wrong answer came from the most expensive model); judgment tasks did not. Two 2026 surveys confirm nobody ships memory-learned routing yet. The routing study.
Trade-off knob Index compression ("diet SPINE"). Compressing the always-loaded knowledge index to a third of its size saved real tokens but measurably lost retrieval recall on paraphrased requests (10/12 vs 12/12 held-out). By the release rule it did NOT ship — if it ever does, it will be opt-in with this exact consequence stated.
Corrected Naive savings accounting. Our first friction measurements over-counted 3–10x (reading a knowledge file that describes an error puts its text in the conversation). Published as a methodology warning so nobody repeats it — including us.
In research Does memory pay for itself? Honest current answer: distill's overhead is proven small (~5–6% of spend); its savings are directional but not yet counterfactually measured. The benchmark is being instrumented to log tokens so this becomes a number, not a belief. Falsifiable claims only.

Your data

This feature collects nothing. No telemetry, no phoning home, no analytics — the presets are two markdown files on your disk, and every number on this page comes from the maintainer's own usage, published voluntarily with personal data scrubbed. That's the standing policy for the whole token-saving line: measurements happen on our machines, not yours.

If distill ever proposes usage telemetry, it will follow the same rule as every trade-off knob: explicit opt-in, aggregate-only, published schema, and you can read exactly what would be sent before agreeing. Nothing about your knowledge base, your code, or your prompts would ever qualify.

Full control

The feature is on after updating (it changes nothing about distill's behavior — only what helpers carry). Your choice persists across updates:

# macOS / Linux
curl -sL .../install.sh | bash -s -- --remove-token-saver  # remove the presets
curl -sL .../install.sh | bash -s -- --token-saver         # bring them back

# Windows (PowerShell)
$env:DISTILL_TOKEN_SAVER='remove'; irm .../install.ps1 | iex
$env:DISTILL_TOKEN_SAVER='on';     irm .../install.ps1 | iex

# or just delete the files — they're yours
~/.claude/agents/scribe.md   ~/.claude/agents/scout.md

The installer never overwrites an agents/scribe.md or agents/scout.md it didn't create — if you already have agents with those names, yours win.
